Stars stay put. Asteroids, comets, nearby stars and variable objects don't. Pick a spot and this tool lines up every SPHEREx picture of it so you can watch what moves.

    What am I looking at?

    Time-lapse

    Every SPHEREx image of this spot, aligned star-on-star and played in date order. Anything that moves or flickers stands out.

    Blink

    The classic way Clyde Tombaugh found Pluto: flip between two dates and let your eye catch the jump.

    Difference

    Each frame minus the median of all frames. Unchanging stars vanish; red means brighter than usual, blue means fainter.

    Scan for changes

    Lets the computer do the first pass: it flags anything that appears or brightens, throws out one-off glitches, and names the known asteroids. Whatever's left is worth a human look.

    Motion trail

    All the “extra light” from every date in one picture, coloured by time (purple → red). A moving object leaves a rainbow track.

    SPHEREx sees each patch of sky through a filter whose colour changes across the detector, so the same star can look slightly brighter or fainter between visits simply because it was seen at a different wavelength. Six detectors (D1–D6) cover 0.75–5 µm. Data: NASA/JPL-Caltech SPHEREx Quick Release, served by NASA/IPAC IRSA. Name lookup by CDS Sesame. Not an official NASA product. Source on GitHub.
